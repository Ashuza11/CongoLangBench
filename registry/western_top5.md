# Western DRC top five

Selection combines regional importance with bitext availability. The completed
national Kituba track represents the regional lingua franca without duplicating
its files.

| Rank | Language | ISO | Usable bitext | Status |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Kituba / Kikongo ya Leta | `ktu` | 2,469 processed pairs | Reuse completed national track |
| 2 | Yaka | `yaf` | 7,104 French pairs | Curated; DRC--Angola source label retained |
| 3 | Kikongo / Koongo | `kon` | 315,959 English pairs | Curated; exact source variety unspecified |
| 4 | Yansi / Yanzi | `yns` | 7,951 French pairs | Curated DRC track |
| 5 | Yombe / Kiyombe | `yom` | 10,408 French pairs | Curated DRC track |

All five language tracks exceed the 1,500-pair target. Kituba (`ktu`) and
Kikongo (`kon`) remain separate. The African Languages Lab data uses the broad
target label `kikongo`, so it is relevant to the Western DRC Kongo language
area but is not relabelled as a specific DRC subvariety.

Pende (`pem`) remains the first Western acquisition-backlog language. Searches
of OPUS, MaLA, African Languages Lab `multi-open`, GlotLID, ASJP, and the
installed `africa-bitext-builder` registry found no openly downloadable
Pende--French or Pende--English sentence corpus. Future searches must continue
across humanitarian, education, health, localization, government, dictionary,
subtitle, and community-publication data rather than being limited to Bible
text.
